Family money conversations

Money lessons are coming to classrooms. Here’s one to start at home.

Published 23 November 2026 · 3 minute read

Children make choices about money long before they open an account of their own. A birthday card with cash inside, a question about the price of a toy, or a trip to the shops can all start a conversation.

On 22 November, the Department for Education set out plans to give money skills more space in England’s national curriculum. The announcement describes primary lessons on everyday spending and value, and secondary lessons on topics including rising prices, how savings grow and scams. The detail is still being developed and tested, with teaching aimed for 2028. These lessons are planned, not in classrooms yet.

Try a three-question birthday conversation

If a child receives money as a gift, keep the talk small and concrete:

  • What would you enjoy with some of it now? Let them name something that matters to them.
  • Is there something you might want later? It could be next month or years away. They do not need a perfect answer.
  • What could we keep aside? Choose an amount together, if that suits your family.

There is room for a present they can enjoy today and money they choose to keep for later. The useful lesson is that every pound has a choice attached to it. You can return to the conversation as they get older.

Keep the explanation honest

For younger children, “keeping some for later” is enough. Older children might ask where saved money goes or whether it can grow. Explain the actual account your family uses, if you have one. Interest rates can change, and investments can rise or fall in value. Avoid promising an outcome.

The curriculum announcement is about England. Schools and families elsewhere in the UK may follow different arrangements.

Source: Department for Education, “Pupils learn money skills for real life in curriculum shake-up”, 22 November 2026.
