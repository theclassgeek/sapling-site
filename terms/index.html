4. Junior ISA essentials

A Junior ISA is governed by law and by the chosen provider’s own terms. Money placed in a Junior ISA belongs to the child. A child may take control of the account at 16 and normally cannot withdraw the money until 18. Annual subscription limits apply across the child’s Junior ISAs and can change; the registered contact is responsible for ensuring contributions remain within the applicable limit.

At 18, a Junior ISA normally becomes an adult ISA automatically. The child can then withdraw some or all of the money, leave it in that adult ISA or transfer the adult ISA to another provider under the applicable ISA and provider rules.

A child cannot hold a Junior ISA and a Child Trust Fund at the same time. A Child Trust Fund generally needs to be transferred before a Junior ISA can be opened. Provider eligibility, acceptance, investment choice, charges, transfers and withdrawals are governed by the provider, not these terms.
